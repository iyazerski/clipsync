clipsync keeps your clipboard in sync across Mac, Windows, and Android. It's free, works over any network, and doesn't need an account.

Works anywhere

Your devices don't need to share Wi-Fi. Home, office, and mobile data all work.

Private

Skips passwords

On Mac and Windows, copies your password manager marks as private stay on that device.

No account

No sign-up, no email, no tracking. Free and open source under the MIT license.

How it works

  1. Install clipsync on your computer and click Set up my first device.
  2. Click Add device. Type the short code on your other computer, or scan the QR code with your phone.
  3. Copy text as usual. It shows up on your other devices right away.

Questions

Is my clipboard private?

Yes. Your devices share a key and encrypt every copy with AES-256-GCM before sending it. The relay server only passes encrypted data along. It keeps the latest copy in memory for 2 minutes and can't read it.

Why do I see a warning when I open the app?

The app isn't signed by Apple or Microsoft yet. On a Mac, open the app, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. On Windows, click More info → Run anyway (or use the PowerShell command below, which skips it).

You can also install from the command line:

Mac (Homebrew) 
brew install --cask iyazerski/tap/clipsync
Windows (PowerShell) 
irm https://github.com/iyazerski/clipsync/releases/latest/download/install.ps1 | iex

Why does sending from Android take a tap?

Android doesn't let apps read the clipboard in the background. Copies from your computers arrive on your phone automatically. To send from your phone, select text and pick Send to computer, share it to clipsync, or tap the Send clipboard tile in Quick Settings.

Does it sync images or files?

No. Text only, up to 256 KB. There's no history, and the latest copy wins.

Does it work on iPhone or Linux?

Not yet. clipsync runs on macOS 13+, Windows, and Android 14+.

Can I run my own server?

Yes. By default clipsync uses a free public relay at clip.iyazerski.dev, which has no uptime guarantee. You can run your own with Docker. See the self-hosting guide.
